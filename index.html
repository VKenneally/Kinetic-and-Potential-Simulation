Kinetic energy

The energy an object has because it is moving.

A rolling ball, a running person and a vibrating speaker cone all have kinetic energy.

For the same object, faster movement means more kinetic energy.
⌁

Potential energy

Stored energy due to an object's position or shape.

Gravitational: an object raised above a reference level.
Elastic: a spring or elastic object stretched or compressed.

Chemical energy is also stored energy, associated with substances such as food and fuel.
An object can have both. A ball falling towards the ground is moving AND is still above the ground. It has kinetic and gravitational potential energy.

Energy changes form. It does not disappear.

A raised ball can speed up as it falls: gravitational potential energy decreases while kinetic energy increases. Friction can transfer energy to the thermal stores of objects and their surroundings.

Electrical energy transfers energy to devices. Devices can produce movement, heating, light or sound. Sound transfers energy through vibrations.
